Rural transports are long, and the radio report is rushed.

On a long rural run the report to the receiving hospital gets made while the crew is still treating the patient. It takes about thirty seconds to say, and the ED uses it to have the right people and equipment waiting at the door.

The part that goes wrong usually isn't the connection. It's that something gets left out because it didn't seem worth saying at the time, and the ED asks for it after the patient is already through the doors. A rural EMS officer in eastern Nebraska described exactly this when we asked him how his service structures the call, and it changed what we built.

Enter fragments as you go

Short, out of order, in your own shorthand. The tool timestamps each one so the sequence stays intact.

Get it back in your order

Unit and code, age and sex, mental status, where you came from, complaint with onset, assessment findings, treatment, vitals with trends, ETA. Your service's order, not a format someone else picked.

See what's missing before you key up

The tool checks the report against the things an ED most often asks for afterward and flags the gaps while you can still do something about them.

What it won't do.

This is the part that matters most, so it's written down rather than buried in a disclaimer.

  • It doesn't diagnose. No impressions, no differentials, no suggested working diagnosis.
  • It doesn't advise on treatment. It won't recommend an intervention, question one you made, or flag a dose.
  • It doesn't add anything you didn't say. A field with no data stays empty and gets marked as not entered. It never fills a gap with a plausible number.
  • It doesn't send on its own. You read the report, you change anything that's wrong, and nothing leaves the tablet until you approve it.

Where this actually stands.

It's a working prototype

Not a finished product and not in service anywhere. It runs in a browser, it works today, and it's being tested against synthetic cases to check that it never invents information.

Built in Omaha by two students

Rewritten once already after a rural EMS officer explained how the report actually works at his service. We'd rather be corrected early than be wrong for a year.

Free for rural and volunteer services

Volunteer squads can't buy the commercial products in this space. That's most of the reason this exists.

We want to be told it's wrong

If you run calls and the output doesn't match how your service does it, that's the most useful thing you could tell us.
